Students skip meals because the information isn't there

Students at the University of Maryland need a quick, reliable way to get affordable food on and near campus. Instead, they run into unreliable dining hours, an awkward campus layout, and no clear idea how long a wait or walk will take.

Without reliable information, students spend their breaks walking to places that turn out to be closed, or give up and skip the meal altogether.

A typical 25-minute break

  1. 12:05
    Class lets out A café's old web page says it's open until 2 p.m
  2. 12:14
    Arrives to a closed sign Hours changed this semester
  3. 12:20
    Checks the dining hall The line is out the door
  4. 12:30
    Walks into the next class hungry Lunch skipped again

Illustrative scenario. We'll replace it with stories from our user research.

    Problem domain

    • Campus life & student well-being
    • Food & dining navigation
    • Mobile productivity
    • Local discovery

    Who we're designing for

    Mostly UMD students, plus the food businesses that serve them.

    🏫

    On-campus students

    Moving between dorms, classes, and dining halls on foot, often with only a few minutes between them.

    🚌

    Commuters

    On campus for long stretches with no kitchen nearby and no time to go home between classes.

    🍜

    Local restaurants & campus food services

    College Park businesses and on-campus vendors who want to reach the student market.

    Stakeholder

    What we're designing around

    Any solution has to work within the limits students already live with.

    ⏱Short breaks
    A meal has to fit in the gap between two classes.
    💸Tight budgets
    Price matters as much as distance. A dining hall lunch at the door is $16.25.
    🚶Walking only
    Many students can't drive, so anything far off campus is out.
    🕘Hours that change
    Hours vary by day and by semester, and some venues are still listed as "TBD".

    Project milestones

    The five stages of our project. We'll post each deliverable here as we finish it.

    1. Problem Definition & Research Plan

      Problem statement, domain, audience, constraints, and how we'll study them.

    2. User Research & Synthesis

      Interviews and surveys with UMD students, then an affinity diagram of what we learn.

    3. Ideation & Prototype

      Sketches, then an interactive prototype of TerpBites.

    4. Usability Testing & Iteration

      Testing the prototype with students and fixing what trips them up.

    5. Final Portfolio & Presentation

      The finished design, the case for it, and our final presentation.
