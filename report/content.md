## Introduction

The idea behind TerpBites is to create an online portal that allows University of Maryland (UMD) students to easily access information about dining options on campus during their short breaks. UMD has three dining halls, as well as a number of cafés, food courts, and markets around campus. Upon reviewing public dining hours for UMD, we discovered that there are a total of 32 different dining venues that operate with differing hours. Many students have trouble figuring out where they can eat during short breaks in their classes. TerpBites allows students to find all the information about food venues, including hours of operation, prices, menus, and travel time to reach the venue in one place.

## Problem statement

University of Maryland students require an effective and quick solution to find low-cost meals on campus and nearby. Currently, students are having a hard time coping with unpredictable dining hours, campus location difficulties, and having no idea regarding the overall time it would take them to wait for a meal. The absence of reliable information results in students wasting their breaks, going all the way only to find that the restaurants are closed or that they eventually have no meal.

## Research questions

1. **RQ1** How do UMD students currently decide where to eat between classes?
2. **RQ2** What information matters most to students when they have limited time to get food?
3. **RQ3** What problems do students run into with dining hours, prices, distance, and wait times?
4. **RQ4** How could TerpBites make finding food between classes faster and easier?

## Conclusions

### What we learned

- **Time is the main problem, not food.** Of the survey respondents, **87%** did not eat or postponed eating one meal within the last two weeks because of the issue of time, with the most common open-ended question being which lines or wait times to expect before reaching the venue, as the interviewees mentioned that they would spend 20 minutes or longer trying to find their eating place or meal once they arrive.
- **Students pick by distance and menu.** Distance has been reported as the most significant aspect in the survey (39%), with almost half of the respondents stating they would only walk for between 5 and 9 minutes. Apart from distance, the respondents also emphasized other factors for making a decision, including the menu. In addition, the answers from the survey show that there is a concentration of food in the Stamp and Kirwan.
- **The information exists but students don't use it.** **65%** of respondents use Google Maps as a reference to locate dining facilities, and only 9% make use of the UMD Dining website to determine when the dining facility is open. 48% of respondents indicated that they have gone to a restaurant that was closed, but none named the opening hours as the most important factor in deciding where to eat.
- **Price, lines, and menus are the main things that go wrong.** Distance, price, lines, and menu selection were all mentioned as influencing factors by the respondents. According to the survey, approximately **74%** of respondents agree that price has at least some effect on their decision-making process and one participant noted that a lot of establishments on Stamp are not able to accept Dining Dollars.
- **Students want three things from TerpBites:** walking distance, prices, and estimated wait times.

### Unanswered questions

- For our research, we should investigate the waiting times at different locations. The estimate of around 20+ minutes reported by interviewees should be verified. 
- Do commuters behave differently from on-campus students? Out of 42 students interviewed, only 10 of them live on campus (freshmen).
- What's the reason for students preferring Google Maps over the Dining website? Would they trust TerpBites with opening hours? We need to learn that directly in our interviews.
- Will dining staff be inclined to give their insights on wait time and menu? We haven't talked to any of the interested parties so far.

### How this informs brainstorming and prototyping

The findings we obtained directly influenced our 10+10 brainstorming session. Of all the 10 ideas for features we generated, we selected **real-time dining hours**, a **break length filter**, and **current location** based on their conformity with the time, closure, and distance issues identified in our surveys and interviews. Additionally, the interviews demonstrated that students find menus to be essential after they are at the venue, indicating that menus should also be included in the results of the break length filter. We prepared a We Believe statement for each of the ideas generated. For instance, we believe that a break length filter will allow students with busy schedules to find a place to dine properly and be confident in the success of this assumption after they verify their ability to find a meal in the allocated amount of time. The subsequent steps will be fulfilling the rest of our observations, organize the second round of interviews supported by notes, refresh Maya's and Jordan's personas, and develop a clickable prototype combining all three features.
